The obvious fit says tyres get faster as they wear. It’s fuel.

Fit lap time against tyre age on practice long runs — the standard approach — and two of the three compounds come back claiming a worn tyre is quicker than a fresh one. Inside a run the car burns about a kilogram of fuel for every lap the tyre ages, so the two are collinear and the age slope absorbs the fuel effect, which pulls the other way. We estimated fuel sensitivity on race laps instead, where the same tyre age appears at many fuel loads, and imported it.

Earlier pit-step research: leave-one-event-out

Every point is one real pit stop, scored by a model that never saw its event. Ground truth is the step in pace across the stop: tyre age resets, fuel does not, so the step approximates the tyre and compound effect, with residual fuel and traffic differences. That is why we score against this and not against our own fit.

Diagonal is perfect prediction. Calibration slope is the fitted slope of this cloud — 1.0 would mean the magnitudes are exactly right.

Residuals, predicted minus measured. Centred means unbiased on average; the spread is the RMSE.

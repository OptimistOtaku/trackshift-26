# PITWALL: four stages, one race-winning question

About 90 seconds. Follow the four numbered workflow buttons in the console.

**Opening**

“Spain, 2021. Lewis Hamilton is chasing Max Verstappen. Staying close is one thing; finding a way past is another. Mercedes faces a question every race engineer knows: do we stay out, or sacrifice track position for fresher tyres? That is the decision story behind PITWALL.”

**01 — Tyre condition**

“First, understand what the tyres are costing us. A slower lap could mean ageing tyres, traffic or changing conditions. PITWALL brings tyre age, compound and pace-response evidence together, helping the engineer judge whether a tyre change could recover performance.”

**02 — Forecast pace**

“Next, look ahead. Can we keep fighting on this set, or will the gap grow? PITWALL forecasts the next laps with uncertainty, then compares each prediction with the actual lap when it arrives. The question becomes: is staying out still competitive?”

**03 — Plan the stop**

“Mercedes brought Hamilton in again on lap 42 for another set of mediums. He gave up time immediately to gain pace later. PITWALL lets us compare supported compounds and weigh the expected pace benefit against pit loss and warm-up. A faster tyre only helps if it repays the stop.”

**04 — Rejoin & attack**

“But where do we come back out? PITWALL projects rejoin neighbours and lets us test traffic costs and rival response. In Spain, Hamilton chased Verstappen down and passed him on lap 60 to win. The lesson: fresh tyres need enough laps and a workable route back to the rival.”

**Close**

“Understand the tyres. Forecast the pace. Price the stop. Plan the attack. PITWALL connects those four questions so the engineer can see the opportunity—and the assumptions behind it.”

## Presenter context

The Spanish GP is a historical illustration, not a PITWALL simulation or a claim
that Mercedes used this product. The demo uses its own cached race replay;
do not present its figures as Hamilton's 2021 data. PITWALL estimates pace
response, not physical wear percentage. Stop and rejoin outputs are conditional
planning scenarios; optimal pit timing and positions gained are not validated.

Historical reference: [2021 Spanish Grand Prix race report, including the lap-42
second stop and lap-60 lead change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Spanish_Grand_Prix#Race_report).
